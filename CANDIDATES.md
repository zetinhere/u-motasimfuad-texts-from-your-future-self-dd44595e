# Candidate lines — Texts From Your Future Self

Brief (from the published judge brief): one text message, under 200
characters, from the writer's future self, funny by itself with no setup.
The house judge compares pairs and weighs, most important first:

1. Is a funny text message from the writer's future self, sent back to the
   present self (off-topic loses to any on-topic funny text).
2. Lands a real joke, with a twist or a flash of recognition that pays off
   by the last line.
3. Sounds like a specific future self, with a voice and details that could
   only come from that person's life.
4. Uses the 200 characters well, no wasted words, punchline arrives fast.

## Full texts of the finalists

**#2 (winner):** "Future you here: do NOT send that 3am text to your crush. In 2028
she reads it aloud on her podcast to 2 million listeners. Episode one. It's
titled after you."

**#3 (runner-up):** "Hey, it's you from 2038. Stop texting your ex happy birthday
every year — she's married to the guy who invented the thing you keep saying
you'll invent."

## Why #2 wins

- Head-to-head vs #3: equal on voice (C1) and specificity (C3), but #2's
  payoff is four words — "It's titled after you." — where #3's payoff is a
  long clause that takes a beat to parse. In a text message, the faster
  punchline wins (C4), and #2's twist (private text becomes public podcast
  content) is the sharper flash of recognition (C2).
- Fresh angle check vs the visible field (reply-all email, plant, chair,
  haircut, boat, self-checkout apology): no other visible entry is a
  crush-text / podcast line.
- Revision after the first run: the v1 line had no audience size; adding
  "to 2 million listeners" raises the stakes and the specificity (C3) for
  20 extra characters, still well inside the 200-character gate.

## Final line (159 characters)

Future you here: do NOT send that 3am text to your crush. In 2028 she reads
it aloud on her podcast to 2 million listeners. Episode one. It's titled
after you.
